## 3. What the research says is still on the table

- **arXiv 2610.19583 — "Who's Behind the Text? Robust Model Authorship Attribution"**
  (14 Oct 2026). The competition's own method paper, and the source of ATLAS:
  source-conditioned likelihood instead of direct classification, with evidence
  aggregated **at token level and clipped so no single token dominates**. Already in
  our pipeline at 10 % weight.
- **arXiv 2605.19908 — "Where Does Authorship Signal Emerge in Encoder-Based Language
  Models?"** The single most actionable line found so far: *"authorship attribution
  models fine-tuned with the same pretrained encoder, data, and loss can differ
  four-fold in performance depending only on their scoring mechanism… mean pooling
  forces consolidation by early to mid layers, while late interaction defers it to
  later layers."* Every readout in every public notebook, including ours, is
  mean-pooled. Late interaction has never been tried here.
- **arXiv 2607.14905** — reasoning-graph GNN: +27 pp under paraphrase, **+19 pp on
  unseen model versions**, over a Longformer baseline.
- **arXiv 2604.21300 (EAVAE)** — style/content disentanglement via separate encoders
  plus a discriminator; SOTA on PAN21. Content–style entanglement is exactly the
  prompt-leakage problem we fight with centring.
- Discussion **752810** — 36k → 124k pool moved a blend from LB 0.586 → 0.617 (+0.031).
- Discussion **753053** — grouped CV systematically over-picks test-time centring; the
  validated fix is to cluster validation rows *together with the unlabelled test batch*
  and score only the validation rows.

## 4. Constraint that decides the shape of v20

GPU quota **10.74 h** (refreshes 2026-10-31); a submission rerun costs up to 8.3 h.
**Training a new member is impossible.** v20 must be inference-only.

Which members can still be improved at inference time? The sparse ones — they are
**refit inside the notebook** on `--pool`:

| `tfidf` (4-view LR) | 0.25 | yes | same |
| `distorted` (Stamatatos) | 0.10 | yes | same |

The **open pool** (`crtproduction/name-that-llm-open-pool`, 254,561 rows, 18 labels,
245,081 after its own leakage flags) is a strict superset of what the notebooks use,
and it carries `prompt_group` — 24,240 groups with ≥2 rows, 179,591 rows in them.

That gives one lever nobody publishes: **the centred twin is trained on 2,699 official
rows today; the pool supplies 66× more grouped rows for exactly the member type that
exploits within-prompt contrast.** Twins, Gaussian heads and adaptive centring are the
only test-time rules that keep surviving on this LB.

## 5. Measured: the pool-trained centred member (experiment 31)

`experiments/31_pool_centred_twin.py`, `StratifiedGroupKFold(2)` on `prompt_id` over the
2,699 official rows, 40k open-pool rows, CPU only, four TF-IDF views identical to the
`svc4` member. Mean balanced accuracy over the two folds:

| member | fit rows | BA | Δ vs plain official |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A plain, official only | 1,350 | 0.3575 | — |
| B plain, official + pool (= `svc4` today, at 120k) | 41,340 | 0.4401 | **+0.083** |
| C centred, official only (the twin shape every notebook uses) | 1,350 | 0.4112 | +0.054 |
| **D centred, official + centred pool** | 41,340 | **0.5001** | **+0.143** |
| E blend of B and D, w = 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0 | — | 0.4638 / 0.4841 / 0.5001 | — |

Three readings:

1. The pool is worth **+0.083** to the plain SVC and **+0.089** to the centred one. This is
   the same effect 752810 measured for a 36k→124k pool (+0.031 there, larger here because
   our baseline has no pool at all).
2. **Centring is worth +0.054 with no pool and +0.143 with one.** The two levers compose;
   neither alone reaches what both give.
3. D beats B by **+0.060** at equal data. The centred member is the stronger single
   classifier of the two, and blending it with B at equal weight is no better than D alone
   (E at w=1.0 = D) — i.e. D dominates B, it is not just decorrelated from it.

Caveat, stated plainly: this is measured at m = 18 answers per prompt with true prompt
ids. Discussion 752708 shows the LB follows the **thin-cluster** columns (m = 2-3), where
heavy centring of a strong member *loses* 0.03-0.05. So the +0.060 will not survive
intact. That is why v20 gives the new member a nominal weight of 1.0 against `svc4`'s 1.5
(relative 0.4, i.e. the E w≈0.4 row, +0.034 at member level) and only scores it on test
rows that fall inside inferred clusters of 2-18.

## 6. Blend arithmetic: why inference-only changes are capped

In the 0.660 blend the weights are q14v5 3.0, n14b 2.5, q8v5 2.0, ll8v5 1.0, q25_14 1.0,
deb 1.0, svc4 1.5, tfidf 0.25, distort 0.10, twins 3.33 (gated to low-margin rows),
ATLAS 0.10, comp 0.15. The six frozen LoRA/encoder members are **10.5 of ~12.4**, i.e.
~85 % of the evidence, and none of them can be changed without GPU training.

So the refittable share is ~12 %: a member-level gain of +0.034 on `svc4` is worth
**≈ +0.004** on the blend. That is the whole ceiling of an inference-only submission, and
it is why v20 is expected at 0.665-0.672 rather than 0.70.

## 7. What is actually left

Everything the public lineage has already tried and measured is inside 0.660. The
remaining levers all need training, and the quota refreshes on 2026-10-31:

1. **New diverse members.** Discussion 753177 is the cleanest evidence: a member whose
   OOF rose 0.569 → 0.605 made the LB fall 0.651 → 0.647, i.e. *diversity beats strength*
   once a member is trained on the same pool as its siblings. A backbone the ensemble does
   not have (OLMo-2, Gemma, Mistral) is worth more than a better Qwen.
2. **Late-interaction scoring** (arXiv 2605.19908): same encoder, data and loss differ
   four-fold by scoring mechanism. Every readout here is mean-pooled.
3. **A per-member OOF harness** so blend changes stop being guesses: we have no saved OOF
   log-probabilities for the frozen members, so every weight decision today is blind.
